01 — Foundations

Two colours held apart
by a third that shouts.

Where the gold program whispers, this one declares. Black and white do the structure; red is spent like capital — one gesture per composition, never a gradient, never a wash. The marks are drawn bolder to carry it: heavier strokes, larger fields, harder edges.

Position

The studio with a spine. Confident to the point of bluntness, never loud for its own sake.

Audience

People who screenshot posters. Buyers who want the work to take a side.

Rule

Red appears once per frame. If it appears twice, one of them is wrong.

02 — The Five Directions

Five marks.
Harder edges.

New geometry, not recolours. Each mark is built to survive one-colour print, cut vinyl, and a 16-pixel square — because a red-and-black system gets reproduced cheaply, and cheap reproduction is where identities die.

ITERATION V2·01

Vantage

A white peak with one red line of descent from the summit. Ambition, with the route drawn in. The most poster-ready of the five: at billboard scale the red line reads from a moving car, at 16px the silhouette still holds.

ITERATION V2·02

Signal Core

Three arcs opening from a red core: the lab ping, reduced to its physics. The red dot is the only filled element in the mark, which makes it the thing the eye lands on first and last. Reads as broadcast, research, reach.

ITERATION V2·03

Counter L

A heavy L with a red counter floating in the notch — the pause inside the work. Typographers call the enclosed space a counter; this one is pulled out, coloured, and made the signature. The heaviest, most ownable letterform in either program.

04 — Recommendation

The verdict.

Primary mark — recommended

V2·03 · Counter L

The strongest letterform in either program. The red counter is a signature that survives every reproduction method, including the cheap ones. It carries the card, the box, the wall, and the favicon without translation.

Campaign mark — supporting

V2·01 · Vantage

Paired with Counter L for out-of-home and campaign moments. Where Counter L is the signature, Vantage is the statement: billboards, posters, launch films. One system, two volumes — black-and-red spoken softly, then spoken plainly.
